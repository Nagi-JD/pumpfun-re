# Instruction-data frames of the deployed `pump` and `pump_amm` programs

Anchor strips the 8-byte instruction discriminator before the handler runs: every handler works on a reader over the instruction bytes that follow it. This page records, for each of the 56 `pump` and 38 `pump_amm` deployed handlers, what the code does with that reader - where the reader comes from, the length gates the bytes are held to, the byte offset and width of every load, and the Borsh length prefixes and element caps of the variable-length arguments.

Generated by `tools/trade_arg_frames.py`; the raw records are `maps/pump_arg_frames.json` and `maps/pump_amm_arg_frames.json`. Every figure is an instruction index in the deployed ELF (`insn = byte_offset / 8`).

## How a frame is recovered

* **Handler attribution comes from the dispatcher.** `disc = sha256("global:" + name)[:8]` is carried as a little-endian `lddw` immediate and compared with the register form of `jeq` (op `0x1d`); the branch reaches a stub, and the stub reaches the body with a call or a jump. That resolves 56 `pump` and 38 `pump_amm` handlers from the bytes, matching the IDL one for one.
* **Two reader regimes.** A handler that reads its own arguments holds the caller frame pointer in `r5`: the reader pointer is `[r5-0x1000]` and the remaining length `[r5-0xff8]`. A handler that delegates stashes the pointer in a frame slot and calls the generated deserialiser with `r2 = fp+S`, i.e. `&mut &[u8]` (pointer at `[r2]`, length at `[r2+8]`).
* **The delegated frame sees an already advanced reader**, so its offsets are relative to the position the handler handed over. That base is carried per frame: `create_config` hands the reader at `16` (after two `u64`s) and `create_pool` at `52` (after the `bool` and one option tag), and the loads inside those frames are placed at `base + off`.
* **Length gates** are the `jlt`/`jeq` immediates taken against the remaining length: `jlt N` when N bytes are taken at once, `jeq 0` when a single byte is read (a non-empty reader suffices), and `jeq N`/`jne N` when an exactly-N-byte frame selects an optional tail.
* **A `Pubkey` is not one load.** It arrives as a 32-byte inlined copy spread over seven loads (`ldxw@0`, `ldxh@+4`, `ldxdw@+6`, `ldxb@+14`, `ldxdw@+15`, `ldxdw@+23`, `ldxb@+31`), so the spans below are merged from the loads rather than read off one instruction.
* **Loads after an advance** are reported, not guessed: once a callee has moved the reader the offset of a later load is run-time, and the record counts those loads separately instead of placing them.

### Counts

* 21 handlers declare no arguments, and their bytes agree: the reader is never loaded.
* 31 handlers load the argument bytes in their own frame and 4 hand the reader to a deserialiser frame.
* 28 handlers reproduce the declared frame completely - every declared field is loaded at the offset the IDL implies.
* 6 handlers carry at least one variable-length argument (`admin_cto`, `create`, `create_v2`, `distribute_fee_to_holders`, `multi_hop_curve_swap`, `update_buyback_config`).
* 3 handlers read past a reader advance, where offsets are run-time: `create`, `create_v2`, `set_params`.

### The one-byte frame and the option tag

5 handlers take a single one-byte argument (`distribute_creator_fees_v2`, `set_max_curve_depth`, `toggle_cashback_enabled`, `toggle_create_v2`, `toggle_mayhem_mode`) and gate it with `jeq 0`: a one-byte read only needs a non-empty reader, so there is no `jlt`.

`buy` (0x2549c) is the clearest option frame: `jlt 8@0x254a6` for the first `u64`, then `jne 16@0x254ad` and `jeq 17@0x254e5` select the optional tail, and the four loads land at `ldxdw@0, ldxdw@8, ldxb@16, ldxb@17` - 16 bytes of amounts plus one tag byte per `OptionBool`, 18 bytes in total.

The same shape repeats across the trade family: `buy_v2`/`buy_v3`/`buy_exact_quote_in_v3` take 17 bytes (two `u64`s and one tag, `jeq 16`), while `buy`/`buy_exact_sol_in` take 18 (two tags, `jne 16` then `jeq 17`). The tag byte is loaded, not inferred: `ldxb@16` and `ldxb@17` are in the record.

### Variable-length arguments

`create` (0x24c1b) declares three `String`s and a `Pubkey`. The `u32` length prefix of the first string is a 4-byte load at offset 0 and the gate in the shared string reader is `jlt 4@0x22ed`; the element cap is `jlt 1048576@0x22f7`, i.e. a 1 MiB bound applied to the prefix before the allocation. After the reader moves, the three strings are consumed by that reader and the `creator` key is loaded from the advanced reader, so 7 loads in the frame are recorded without a static offset.

`distribute_fee_to_holders` (0x258fa) takes a `Vec<u64>`: the measured frame is the 4-byte length prefix at offset 0 (`0+4`), and the elements follow at a run-time offset.
`update_buyback_config` (0x263d6) declares one `Option<u64>` and measures 9 bytes: the 1-byte tag plus the 8-byte payload, both placed (`0+9`).

### Pubkey arguments

`update_holder_reward_config` (0x244a2) is `bool` + `Pubkey`: 33 bytes, and the record holds the seven fragment loads that make up the key (`ldxh@1+2, ldxw@3+4, ldxdw@7+8, ldxb@15+1, ldxdw@16+8, ldxdw@24+8, ldxb@32+1`).

`set_params` (0x2494e) is the deepest frame in the program: 72 bytes of `u64` and `Pubkey` fields loaded at placed offsets, the `bool` at 72 read through the option reader `0x1a0d` (gate `jeq 0@0x1a0f`), and the four remaining fields behind that advance; the record counts 2 loads past it.

### The 8-pubkey array

`create_config` and `update_fee_config` share `0x1b1e`. The handler loads `lp_fee_basis_points` at 0 and `protocol_fee_basis_points` at 8 in its own frame, then hands the reader at 16 to the array reader, whose per-element gate is `jlt 32@0x1b20`: the 8 x 32 bytes land at 16..272, and the measured span is 0+272. The two trailing fields are read after the array advances the reader (8 loads in the record).

`create_pool` (0x1c634) is the widest frame in either program: `u16` at 0, two `u64`s at 2 and 10, the `coin_creator` key across 18..50, the `bool` at 50 (`ldxb@50`), the first option tag at 51 (`ldxb@51`), an 8-byte load at 52 under a 9-byte gate (`jlt 9`), then the reader advanced to 60 where the two trailing `OptionBool`s are read through `0xde96`. Measured span 0+60, all declared fields loaded.

### The one-byte gates

3 `pump_amm` handlers take a single one-byte `bool` (`toggle_boost`, `toggle_cashback_enabled`, `toggle_mayhem_mode`) and gate it with `jeq 0`.

`disable` (0x1c2a1) is five bools, and the gate immediates count them: jeq 0@0x1c2ab, jeq 1@0x1c2c9, jeq 2@0x1c2ce, jeq 3@0x1c2d3, jeq 4@0x1c2d8. Each `jeq N` requires the remaining reader to hold N bytes, so the fifth gate is the 5-byte frame.

### Option payloads widen the frame

`admin_cto_pool` (0x1d5fe) measures 42 bytes where the tag-only layout is 34: `Pubkey` 0..32, `bool` at 32, a tag at 33 and the `OptionU64` payload at 34..42. `create_pool` shows the same effect at its tail, and `update_buyback_config` in both programs is the minimal case at 9 bytes (tag + `u64`).

## The two programs side by side

* `pump` has 31 own-frame, 4 delegated and 21 argument-free handlers; `pump_amm` 19, 3 and 16.
* Both programs load the reader from the same two places: `[r5-0x1000]`/`[r5-0xff8]` in the handler frame, `[r2]`/`[r2+8]` in a deserialiser frame. The delegated frames are per argument-struct, so two handlers that declare the same argument list share one.
* `pump` is the program with variable-length arguments: three `String`s in `create`, `bytes` in `multi_hop_curve_swap`, `Vec<u64>` in `distribute_fee_to_holders`, and the 1 MiB string cap. `pump_amm` arguments are fixed-width or `Option`, so it carries no length prefix at all.
* `pump` measures 186 loads and 48 gates; `pump_amm` 206 and 34.
